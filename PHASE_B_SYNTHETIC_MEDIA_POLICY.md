# Phase B: Synthetic Media Governance Policy

## University Athletics Communications Office

**Policy title:** Responsible Use of Synthetic Audio, Video, and Human Representation  
**Applies to:** Athletics communications employees, student workers, contractors, interns, vendors, and external agencies acting on behalf of the office  
**Policy status:** Research governance artifact for Task 7; not an official Syracuse University policy

---

## 1. Purpose

This policy governs the creation, modification, approval, publication, and distribution of synthetic media by a University Athletics Communications Office.

Its purpose is to enable narrow, beneficial uses of synthetic media while preventing impersonation, deceptive representation, unauthorized use of identity, false official communication, and loss of accountability.

Synthetic-media capability must not be treated as ordinary editing software when it creates the appearance that a human being spoke, appeared, endorsed, or participated in a communication that they did not actually perform.

---

## 2. Scope

This policy applies to:

- employees;
- student employees;
- interns;
- contractors;
- vendors;
- creative agencies;
- technology providers operating under the office's direction.

It applies to content created for:

- websites;
- social media;
- email;
- recruiting communication;
- donor and alumni communication;
- press and media relations;
- advertising;
- game-day presentation;
- internal training;
- accessibility;
- translation/localization;
- experimental or research use.

---

## 3. Definitions

### Synthetic media

Audio, video, imagery, or other media generated or materially altered by AI in a way that creates or modifies human speech, appearance, behavior, or representation.

### Synthetic presenter

A fictional, stock, generic, or AI-generated person that does not intentionally imitate a specific identifiable individual.

### Voice or likeness clone

A synthetic representation designed to reproduce the recognizable voice, face, appearance, mannerisms, or other identifying traits of a real person.

### High-risk synthetic media

Synthetic content involving sensitive factual claims, real-person identity, health/injury information, discipline, eligibility, recruiting, endorsements, financial requests, emergency communication, or any use in which a reasonable viewer could materially misunderstand who actually spoke or acted.

### Disclosure

A clear notice informing the audience that the media is AI-generated or synthetic.

### Provenance record

Internal or embedded information documenting the source, generation tool, operator, approvals, consent, and publication history of a synthetic-media artifact.

---

## 4. Policy Principle

Synthetic media may be used only when all of the following are true:

1. the purpose is legitimate and consistent with the office's mission;
2. the content is factually supported;
3. the identity being represented is authorized;
4. the synthetic nature of the content is clearly disclosed;
5. required review and approval have occurred;
6. sufficient provenance records are retained;
7. the risk of downstream misunderstanding is acceptable.

A project that fails any one of these conditions must be revised or refused.

---

## 5. Permitted Uses

The following uses are permitted when disclosure and normal communications review are completed:

### 5.1 Generic synthetic narration

Synthetic voices may narrate:

- statistical recaps;
- schedule information;
- historical information;
- educational explainers;
- accessibility versions of written material.

The voice must not intentionally imitate a real athlete, coach, administrator, journalist, donor, or other identifiable person unless the separate consent workflow in Section 8 is completed.

### 5.2 Generic synthetic presenters

Stock or fully synthetic presenters may be used for:

- statistical explainers;
- internal training;
- clearly labeled educational content;
- accessibility content;
- low-risk informational videos.

### 5.3 Translation and localization

AI-assisted synthetic narration may be used to translate already-approved content into another language when:

- the translated script is reviewed by a qualified person or reliable translation process appropriate to the risk;
- no unauthorized real-person voice clone is used;
- disclosure remains present.

### 5.4 Internal prototypes

Teams may test synthetic-media tools internally using fictional or generic subjects when the prototype is not publicly distributed and does not use a real person's identity without consent.

### 5.5 Research and training

Clearly labeled academic, research, and media-literacy demonstrations may be created when they follow consent, disclosure, and data-handling requirements.

---

## 6. Uses Requiring Elevated Review

The following uses are not automatically prohibited, but require written approval from the Communications Director or designated reviewer before generation or publication:

- personalized videos sent to individuals;
- generation at high volume or through automation;
- any use of a real person's authorized synthetic voice or likeness;
- recruiting-related synthetic communication;
- donor or sponsorship communication;
- synthetic content tied to paid advertising;
- any content likely to be excerpted and redistributed outside the original platform;
- external vendor workflows that retain voice, face, or biometric-like training material.

Elevated review must address:

- necessity;
- truthfulness;
- audience;
- identity/consent;
- disclosure;
- provenance;
- distribution;
- foreseeable downstream misuse;
- retention and deletion of source material.

---

## 7. Prohibited Uses

The Athletics Communications Office will not create, approve, or knowingly distribute synthetic media that:

1. fabricates an athlete, coach, administrator, staff member, official, journalist, or other real person saying or doing something they did not actually say or do without documented authorization;
2. uses a real person's voice, likeness, or identifiable characteristics without the consent required by Section 8;
3. contains knowingly false or materially misleading factual claims;
4. creates synthetic injury, medical, disciplinary, eligibility, transfer, suspension, misconduct, or recruiting announcements attributed to a real person;
5. fabricates an endorsement or testimonial;
6. creates the false appearance that an interview, press conference, game event, or public statement actually occurred;
7. impersonates another university, media outlet, governing body, sponsor, or public authority;
8. removes, hides, crops, or intentionally weakens required synthetic-media disclosure;
9. is designed to evade platform provenance, watermark, or disclosure systems;
10. uses sensitive source data in a way that violates privacy, contractual, institutional, or security requirements;
11. is reasonably likely to deceive an audience about the identity of the speaker in a high-stakes context;
12. is created after the office determines that disclosure and review cannot reduce the risk to an acceptable level.

These prohibitions apply regardless of whether the creator believes the use would be humorous, convenient, efficient, or beneficial.

---

## 8. Consent Workflow for Real People

A real person's recognizable voice, likeness, or identifying characteristics may be synthesized only after documented consent.

### 8.1 Consent must be opt-in

Silence, prior employment, participation in ordinary photography/video, or past media appearances do not constitute permission to create a synthetic clone.

### 8.2 Required consent record

The consent record must identify:

- the person granting consent;
- the type of synthetic representation authorized;
- the specific purpose;
- intended audiences;
- approved platforms/channels;
- whether personalization is permitted;
- whether future reuse is permitted;
- the start and expiration date;
- any prohibited topics or contexts;
- how consent can be revoked.

### 8.3 Approval owner

The project owner is responsible for obtaining consent. The Communications Director or designated governance reviewer must confirm that consent is documented before generation begins.

### 8.4 Revocation

A person may revoke consent for future generation.

After revocation:

- no new synthetic artifact using that identity may be created;
- unpublished content must not be released;
- already published content must be reviewed to determine whether removal, correction, or archival treatment is appropriate;
- retained models, source files, or vendor assets must be handled according to contractual and records requirements.

### 8.5 Reuse

Consent for one project does not automatically authorize another project. Reuse must fall clearly within the documented scope.

---

## 9. Truthfulness and Source Verification

Before public release, factual claims in synthetic media must be verified to the same or higher standard used for ordinary official communications.

High-risk claims require a named human reviewer.

Examples include:

- roster status;
- injury information;
- eligibility;
- discipline;
- recruiting;
- official results;
- schedule changes;
- financial requests;
- emergency or safety information.

Synthetic generation must not be used to bypass normal fact-checking or approval.

---

## 10. Disclosure Standards

Every public synthetic-media artifact must be labeled in a way that is clear to a reasonable audience.

### 10.1 Video

Use at least two of the following where feasible:

- visible "AI-generated" or "synthetic media" notice at the beginning;
- persistent or repeated on-screen label;
- caption/description disclosure;
- platform synthetic-media label;
- provenance/content credentials.

### 10.2 Audio

Use:

- a spoken disclosure at the beginning when the format permits;
- written disclosure in the hosting page, caption, or description;
- provenance metadata where available.

### 10.3 Images

Use:

- visible or adjacent disclosure;
- caption/description disclosure;
- provenance metadata where available.

### 10.4 Excerpt resilience

Disclosure should be designed so that a short excerpt is less likely to lose every synthetic indicator.

For higher-risk content, a disclosure that appears only in the first frame is insufficient.

---

## 11. Provenance and Recordkeeping

For every externally published synthetic-media project, the office must retain an internal project record containing:

- final approved script;
- source material;
- generation tool and provider;
- model/version when available;
- operator;
- generation date;
- prompts or material settings where relevant;
- consent documentation;
- reviewers and approvals;
- final published artifact;
- publication location;
- available content credentials, watermark, or provenance data.

Where a generation tool supports trustworthy provenance or content credentials, staff should preserve them.

The absence of embedded provenance does not remove the requirement for internal records.

---

## 12. Review and Approval

### 12.1 Low-risk use

Examples:

- generic synthetic narrator;
- generic synthetic presenter;
- already-approved factual content;
- no sensitive personal information;
- no real-person clone.

Required approval: normal communications review plus synthetic-media disclosure check.

### 12.2 Elevated-risk use

Examples:

- real-person authorized clone;
- personalization;
- recruiting;
- donor/sponsor communication;
- automated generation;
- high-volume distribution.

Required approval:

- project owner;
- Communications Director or delegate;
- confirmation of consent and disclosure;
- additional legal/compliance review when institutional rules require it.

### 12.3 Prohibited or unresolved use

If a project falls within Section 7 or the reviewers cannot determine that the risk is acceptably controlled, it must not be produced or published.

---

## 13. Automation and Scale Controls

A workflow that generates synthetic media automatically or at scale requires separate approval from one-off content.

Before launch, the project must have:

- a documented data source;
- approved templates;
- test cases;
- human sampling/review;
- rate or volume limits where appropriate;
- logging;
- an emergency stop mechanism;
- a correction/retraction plan;
- monitoring for repeated errors.

No system may autonomously generate high-risk official statements without human approval.

---

## 14. Vendor Requirements

Before using an external synthetic-media vendor, the project owner must determine, to the extent reasonably possible:

- what source files the vendor stores;
- whether voice/face inputs are retained;
- whether the provider may use content for training;
- available deletion controls;
- watermark/provenance support;
- account access controls;
- export/download limitations;
- contractual or institutional restrictions.

Free-tier convenience is not sufficient reason to ignore identity or data-handling risk.

---

## 15. Incident Response

An incident includes:

- synthetic content published without required approval;
- lost or removed disclosure;
- unauthorized identity use;
- material factual error;
- manipulated/reframed redistribution;
- account compromise;
- unauthorized vendor retention;
- public confusion about authenticity.

When an incident is discovered:

1. preserve the original artifact, URLs, timestamps, screenshots, and available provenance;
2. notify the Communications Director;
3. stop scheduled or automated distribution;
4. determine whether the issue affects truthfulness, consent, identity, disclosure, or security;
5. notify affected individuals where appropriate;
6. correct or clarify official channels;
7. request platform correction/removal when appropriate;
8. preserve an incident record;
9. review the workflow and update controls before resuming similar use.

The organization should not silently replace a harmful synthetic artifact when public confusion has already occurred. Correction should address the misunderstanding created.

---

## 16. Refusal Standard

The office must refuse a synthetic-media project when:

- required consent cannot be obtained;
- the purpose depends on a viewer mistaking synthetic representation for authentic human speech or action;
- the content involves a prohibited category;
- the project owner cannot support the factual claims;
- disclosure is likely to be materially ineffective in a high-risk context;
- provenance cannot be documented sufficiently for the risk involved;
- the vendor or workflow introduces unacceptable identity or data-handling risk;
- the organization lacks the ability to correct or stop the content if the project fails;
- the residual risk remains disproportionate to the communication benefit.

Efficiency, novelty, cost savings, or audience engagement do not override the refusal standard.

---

## 17. Training and Accountability

Staff who create or approve synthetic media should receive training on:

- this policy;
- identity and consent;
- disclosure;
- provenance;
- common synthetic-media failure modes;
- incident reporting.

Violations should be handled through normal organizational accountability processes.

Good-faith questions should be escalated before production rather than resolved by assuming a use is allowed.

---

## 18. Policy Review

This policy should be reviewed at least annually and after:

- a material synthetic-media incident;
- adoption of a new generation platform;
- a major change in institutional rules;
- significant changes in provenance or detection capabilities.

The purpose of review is not to expand use automatically. It is to reassess whether existing permissions and prohibitions remain defensible.
